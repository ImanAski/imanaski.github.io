title: Forever almost, forever not

There is a cold spot in me, deep as a winter well, a hollow shaft where every echo descends and never returns, where the possibility of resonance has been extinguished by the very act of listening for it. The chill is not merely physical; it gnaws at the marrow of existence itself, rendering even the pulse a foreign intrusion, as though the heart beats only out of habit, not conviction, a mechanical parody of life. I entertain the absurd fantasy of a shop that trades in hugs—authentic ones, the kind that affirm presence without the mediation of language, arms that enclose like a dialectical necessity, binding the fragmented self into momentary totality. Yet no such establishment exists; the marketplace of human contact offers only counterfeits, and I have long since ceased believing in their currency. It has been an eternity since anyone held me with the gravity of genuine intention, as if I were more than a transient phenomenon in their perceptual field. Should such a moment recur, I would either dissolve into involuntary tears—an eruption of the repressed—or petrify in my customary paralysis, gazing into the void of the other’s eyes and discovering there only my own incapacity for relation, a despair so complete that even despair despairs of itself.

I am exhausted by the infinite repetition of my failures, each collapse a confirmation of the aesthetic sphere’s inadequacy, a self-devouring cycle in which I choose the very suffering I claim to flee. The imagination of embrace is preferable to its actuality, for the former remains under my sovereign control, a safe aesthetic object, while the latter threatens the annihilation of the self I have so carefully preserved in isolation. One more collision with reality would shatter the fragile equilibrium I maintain, like a flame shielded from every draft, yet I know this shielding is itself the sickness: the despair of weakness that refuses to be itself, the despair of finitude that clings to its chains. Thus I address the absent other in a whisper that knows its own futility: where are you, my dragonfly? That ephemeral iridescence, the promise of transcendence that flits just beyond the grasp of the finite. We are all destined for dust, a leveling that renders every striving comic in its pretension, yet I persist in the pretense, hoarding my despair like a miser his coins, unwilling to surrender even the misery that defines me.

This confession itself is nothing but paper, pixels, breath—an ephemeral sign with no signified, a gesture toward relation that collapses into solipsism the moment it is uttered. Nevertheless, I once sat in the rain contemplating the embrace I refused, allowing fear to triumph as it always does, that demonic guardian of the threshold who persuades me that non-existence is preferable to the risk of existence. I claim to await love, but this waiting is itself a choice, a deliberate stasis, hands folded in resignation, refusing the leap that might justify my being. Here is the despair of defiance: I will not become myself, I will not relate, I will not risk the eternal in the temporal; I choose the nothingness I already am, and in this choice I assert my freedom only to negate it, a self that is not a self, a relation that relates to itself by not relating at all.

Yet I cannot help smiling when a child laughs without self-consciousness, a spontaneous eruption that recalls my mother’s lap and her tales of fairies and distant realms—narratives that once sustained the illusion of possibility before I learned to distrust illusion itself. I smile at lovers who abandon themselves to absurdity, indifferent to the gaze of the universal, and in their abandon I glimpse the self I forfeited when I elected the safety of reflection over the terror of decision. Their joy is an accusation: some have escaped the icy grip that now encases me, some have refused to let the cold spot metastasize until it devours the entire topography of the soul. But even this accusation I turn against myself; their freedom becomes my bondage, their authenticity my inauthenticity, their leap my refusal to leap. I envy them, yes, but the envy is itself a form of despair—the despair that wants to be itself in its defiance, yet cannot, because to be itself would require the very relation it rejects.

I continue to observe the clouds, discerning forms—rabbits, ships, dragons—projecting meaning onto the indifferent heavens, but I do not stir. I am the rock, the statue, the monument to potentiality unrealized, arms extended in perpetual deferral, awaiting either erosion by time or the improbable intervention of a hand capable of shattering the frost without destroying what lies beneath. Yet even this waiting is a lie: I do not wait for salvation; I wait for the confirmation that no salvation is possible, that the self is a sickness unto death, that the cold spot is not a wound but the truth of my being. Cold spot, stuck rock, waiting statue—this is the truth of my existence, a self-condemning synthesis of regret and resignation, a despair that despairs of despairing, forever almost, forever not.

I.M. Aski
